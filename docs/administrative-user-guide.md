# Reporting content and requesting review

Use the report action on an accessible recipe, cookbook or named cookbook user. Sign in, select a reason and explain the issue in your own words. A report requests a human review; it does not automatically hide content or penalize an account. A review can find the report unfounded.

Private cookbook content is hidden from unauthorized ordinary users. Specifically authorized platform administrators may use a justified, audited inspection process for legitimate platform administration, security or moderation. Administrator status does not make them cookbook members or give them a general recipe-editing shortcut.

An operator may send a separate decision or review notice to the reporting person or affected account/cookbook owner. The notice contains the operator's public explanation, report/notice references and instructions for contacting the operator to request review. Follow those contact instructions and include the reference. Do not send passwords, verification tokens or unnecessary identity documents. A deactivated account may receive a notice by email even when it cannot sign in.

When an administrator newly restricts a cookbook or an individual recipe, the cookbook owner is automatically notified by email when an address and working mail configuration are available. This email identifies the cookbook or recipe and explains that ordinary access, including owner/editor access, is blocked while the content and history remain preserved. Contact the platform administrator to request review and include the reference. The email includes the administrator’s separate explanation of why access was restricted. Private administrative reasons are not included. The instance operator can customize these messages under **Administration → Settings → Email templates → Cookbook access restricted / Recipe access restricted**. Lifting a recipe or cookbook restriction sends a separate email, optionally including an administrator-authored explanation. Templates are available as **Recipe restriction lifted** and **Cookbook restriction lifted**. Independent restrictions, permissions, publication, visibility and trash state still apply; lifting a restriction does not publish or restore content. Repeated requests without a state change send no email. A cookbook restriction sends one cookbook email without separate emails for its recipes. Mandatory cookbook identity resets do not send these restriction emails. Delivery is attempted once after saving the restriction; failures and missing recipients are recorded in operator logs and do not undo the restriction.

When an administrator deletes a recipe or cookbook, it is permanently removed from the application and is not moved to trash. The cookbook owner is automatically emailed after successful deletion when an address and working mail configuration are available, optionally including the administrator’s separate public explanation. Cookbook deletion sends one summary for the cookbook and its content. Recipe deletion preserves the cookbook and memberships; cookbook deletion preserves user accounts. Templates are **Recipe permanently deleted** and **Cookbook permanently deleted** under the same email settings. Messages identify the removed content by name and UUID. Private administrative reasons are excluded. Delivery failure does not undo deletion; automatic retries are not provided. Ordinary trash moves and retention cleanup do not send these administrative deletion messages.

Ticket decisions and content/account actions are separate. Closing or reopening a report does not itself block, delete, unblock or restore content. A successful request for review requires the operator to perform any appropriate access change explicitly. Previously downloaded files and backups cannot be recalled through a content restriction.

Notice timing and the human review process follow the instance operator's published policy. There is no public appeal-submission portal in the application. If mail is not delivered or instructions are unavailable, use the operator contact published in the instance's legal notice. Technical mail delivery failure does not undo a completed administrative action.
